# One-day scenario: a Tuesday (fictional)

One household, one ordinary Tuesday. Daniel and Lisa are partners, and Timmy
and Mia's parents; Margaret is Daniel's mother, in the circle, living in the
house's second unit; June sits with her on Tuesdays. The scenes below follow
Margaret's day in the second unit and the family's day around it. Every line
in quotes is spoken or written exactly as it appears on the timeline, in the
brief, or in an alert. The coordinator adds nothing of its own to anyone's words.

## 5:40 AM, Daniel leaves for work

Daniel, at the door, one line into his phone:

> "Morning. Depot by nine, hardware store after, home by three. Phone's on me."

Timeline entry, 5:40 AM, from Daniel: "Depot by nine, hardware store after, home by three."

The last part of that line is a promise, and the record treats it as one: a commitment on the timeline, in the words of the person who made it. Households do not mostly fail on forgotten facts; they fail on forgotten promises, when somebody said they would handle it and the person waiting quietly stops believing anyone is coming. Margaret is counting on "home by three." It is why her first question when she wakes unsettled is where he is.

## 7:15 AM, Margaret wakes and asks where Daniel is

Margaret has woken up unsettled and wants her person located.

Margaret: "Where's Daniel?"

Coordinator answers from Daniel's 5:40 AM line, in his words: "Daniel said this morning: depot by nine, the hardware store after, home by three."

Her question leaves no entry of its own. Questions are answered from the record and create nothing; the answer reads Daniel's 5:40 AM line back to her.

## 7:20 AM, spoken lookup and a confirmed media action

Margaret: "And what did he say about Tuesday?"

Coordinator answers from the schedule Daniel set, which he spoke and confirmed on Sunday evening: "Order the refill by Tuesday, so it arrives before the bottle runs out. That is the schedule Daniel set."

Margaret: "Put my program on."

Coordinator proposes the media action and waits: "Your program, on the front room screen?"

Margaret: "Yes."

The program starts. Timeline entries standing after 7:20 AM: Daniel's 5:40 AM line; "Refill: order by Tuesday, so it arrives before the bottle runs out. Schedule set by Daniel."; "Program on. Confirmed by Margaret." Her lookup leaves no entry of its own; the refill line was already on the record from the schedule Daniel set.

## 7:50 AM, the health mention, recorded and handed to a human

This scene is written to play in under 30 seconds. The coordinator records
Margaret's words, asks one question, and sends an alert carrying her words
only. It says nothing about the health content itself.

Margaret: "Oh. My ankle has swollen up again."

Timeline entry, 7:50 AM, Margaret's words, recorded as she said them: "My ankle has swollen up again."

Coordinator: "Written down, in your words. Do you want me to tell Daniel?"

Margaret: "Yes. Tell him now."

Alert to Daniel, at the level Margaret chose in advance, in her own words. His phone carries her words and nothing else:

> Margaret, just now: "My ankle has swollen up again."
> Her word for this one: tell Daniel now.

Two buttons under it: "Got it" and "Call her." Daniel taps "Got it." Timeline entry: "Daniel has it. 7:50 AM."

## 8:05 AM, Daniel reads the morning brief at work

Three lines, exactly as Daniel sees them:

1. Yesterday: everything on the list confirmed done.
2. Today: Lisa at two with the groceries. Quiet afternoon after.
3. Wipes run out around Thursday.

## 9:05 AM, Margaret states her Tuesday bridge club ride

Margaret tells the house her Tuesday plan, in her own words:

> "Daniel is driving me to bridge club on Tuesday, and June is the backup."

Timeline entry, 9:05 AM, from Margaret: "Daniel is driving me to bridge club on Tuesday, and June is the backup."

The line stands Confirmed: it is her statement of the arrangement, recorded as she stated it, and it sits on the same timeline as the rest of the day.

## 12:40 PM, Lisa's one-line capture, at the house over lunch

Lisa is at the house over lunch and has just used the last of the gauze. One line from her phone, at the moment she notices:

> "Last of the gauze going on now."

Timeline entry, 12:40 PM, from Lisa: "Last of the gauze going on now."

The supply picture updates from her line: gauze on hand is now none. New line on the shared record: "Gauze: out. Daniel is assigned to pick up more on his way home."

## 1:15 PM, the sitter uses the by-task household notes

June, who sits with Margaret on Tuesday afternoons, arrives. She taps "Getting dressed" and sees only the three household notes for that task (written in full in task-notes.json): the blue cardigan from the chair, because the green one has the broken button; shoes go on sitting on the bed, not standing; tea after, one sugar, though Margaret will say two.

Timeline entry, 1:15 PM: June arrived. Getting dressed notes opened.

## 2:10 PM, Lisa's second line, out with the groceries

Lisa is out with the groceries, one hand on a bag, phone in the other. One line, at the moment it is true:

> "Opened the last pack of wipes just now."

Timeline entry, 2:10 PM, from Lisa: "Opened the last pack of wipes just now."

Her line corrects the count: the record had counted a sealed pack that is not there. What is left is the open pack, six in it, so at the house's usual rate the forecast moves the moment her line lands: "Wipes run out Wednesday." Nobody had to remember to pass it on; the record holds it for anyone authorized who asks.

## 2:55 PM, the promise lands

Daniel, at the door, one line into his phone:

> "Home, like I said this morning."

Timeline entry, 2:55 PM, from Daniel: "Home, like I said this morning."

Nothing announces it as a promise kept. The morning's commitment and the afternoon's arrival simply stand on the same timeline, five minutes early, where the person who was waiting can see that someone came. A house that remembers promises, and quietly makes sure they land, is a different and better thing than a house that remembers appointments.

## 3:30 PM, Margaret taps instead of talking

Margaret's voice is tired, so this time she does not speak. Daniel is home, but the house habit holds: she does not call down the hall, she tells the house. On the screen of big simple choices she taps "Tell Daniel."

A simple card appears: "Thinking of you. All quiet here." She taps "Send." The note goes to Daniel, the same front door and the same confirmation rule, driven by taps instead of talk.

Timeline entry, 3:30 PM: "Note sent by tap. Confirmed by Margaret."

## 6:30 PM, a note to the house, from no one in particular

Somebody, in passing, out loud:

> "Toilet paper is low."

Timeline entry, 6:30 PM, no author, no subject: "Toilet paper is low."

Events are told to the house, not to a person. This one is not about anybody and does not need to pinpoint who noticed; the house simply holds it, Proposed, until anyone in the circle picks it up and closes it. Attribution is recorded when it is known and matters. Here it is neither, and the record says so honestly instead of inventing a name.

## 8:45 PM, end of day

The day's timeline stands behind her, every entry accounted for: the morning's promise among them, kept at 2:55 PM, and one small item still honestly open, the toilet paper, waiting on whoever reaches a store first. In this house an open item is not a failure. It is tomorrow's first line.

Margaret: "Well. That is the whole day, written down. Goodnight."
