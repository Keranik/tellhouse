# One-day scenario: a Tuesday (fictional)

One household, one ordinary Tuesday, built from the things households most often forget or argue about.
Every line in quotes is exactly what was said, and exactly what the record holds. The house adds nothing of its
own to anyone's words.

## 5:40 AM: Daniel leaves for work
> "Depot by nine, hardware store after, home by three."

The last part is a promise, and the record treats it as one. Households don't mostly fail on forgotten facts;
they fail on forgotten promises, when somebody said they'd handle it and the person waiting quietly stops
believing anyone is coming.

## 6:45 AM: which bin, before the truck
Lisa: "Recycling goes out tonight, the truck comes at seven tomorrow."

## 7:05 AM: the last of a staple, said out loud for once
Lisa: "Used the last of the coffee." The count goes to zero, and Briefing shows coffee as out, with Daniel as the
one who orders it.

## 7:20 AM: the schedule moves
Timmy: "Soccer practice moved to 5:30 tonight."

## 9:05 AM: a shared lift
Margaret: "Daniel is driving me to bridge club on Tuesday, and June is the backup."

## 12:40 PM: a bill, before it is late
Lisa: "Electric bill is due Friday."

## 1:15 PM: the school pickup
June: "Got Mia from school, we're at the house."

## 2:55 PM: the promise lands
Daniel: "Home, like I said this morning." Nothing announces it; the morning's promise and the afternoon's arrival
simply stand on the same timeline, five minutes early, and the claim closes in his own words.

## 3:30 PM: word to Lisa at work
Margaret taps a message through to Lisa: "Mia's homework is done and she's had a snack." It reaches Lisa's inbox,
in Margaret's words, after Margaret says yes.

## 4:50 PM and 5:05 PM: the evening, double-booked
Daniel: "I'll take Timmy to soccer at 5:30." Lisa, fifteen minutes later: "I'll take Timmy to soccer at 5:30."
Neither checked. Both claims stay open on the record, and Briefing says so: the house's own rules notice that
Daniel and Lisa both claimed the same thing, and Muse Wonder, when asked, reviews the same record and cites the
entries it relied on.

## 6:30 PM: a note to the house, from no one in particular
> "Toilet paper is low."

No author, no subject. It is told to the house, not to a person, and it waits, unconfirmed, until anyone closes
it.

## 8:45 PM: end of day
Daniel: "Bins are out, dishwasher's running. Goodnight."
