# Personas (fictional)

The family personas below are invented for the demo, plus June, the named helper in the scenario. No real person's information is used. One household, one property, one shared record: Daniel and Lisa, partners, in the main house with their children Timmy and Mia; Margaret, Daniel's mother, in the house's second unit; June in the circle on Tuesdays.

## Margaret Ellis, 78, Daniel's mother

Margaret lives in the house's second unit and runs her day by talking. She has help around her because some daily tasks need a second pair of hands, but her day takes its direction from her: she states what she needs, asks what she wants to know, and confirms or turns down anything the coordinator proposes before it happens. She is up early, rests after lunch, and asks for her program by name. Most days she talks to the coordinator the way she would talk to someone in the next room. When her voice is tired, she taps big simple choices on the screen instead, and gets the same help either way.

## Daniel, 49, her son, Lisa's partner

Daniel works early warehouse shifts and is out of the house before Margaret wakes, which is exactly why the coordinator matters to him: he leaves a one-line update as he goes out the door, and Margaret can ask where he is and get his own words back. He reads the morning brief at work, acts on the lines that need a person (a refill to order, a pickup on his way home), and confirms what he has done so the timeline stays true. He set Margaret's pill schedule himself, and the coordinator reminds him about refills on the schedule he set. He and Lisa are Timmy and Mia's parents; the afternoon pickup lands with whoever the house's plan names, and the record is where the family sees that it held.

## Lisa, 44, Daniel's partner

Lisa works across town and cannot always be in the house, so she mostly meets the coordinator through her phone: one line at the moment she notices something, a question when she wants to know how the day is going. She is at the house over lunch on Tuesdays and helps in person, and anything she uses up or spots running low goes straight onto the shared record in the same sentence she would have said out loud. Between Daniel's early hours and Lisa's, the household runs on opposite schedules, and the shared timeline is the one place the whole day exists in a single story.

## Timmy, Daniel and Lisa's son

Timmy is the one with soccer at five thirty. His afternoons are the kind that live in one parent's head unless the house holds them: a pickup that has to happen on time, from a place, by whoever the plan names. He is a member of the record in his own right, a subject in his own name, not a line in somebody else's calendar; his parents do the telling for him, the way parents speak for a child everywhere else.

## Mia, Daniel and Lisa's daughter, Timmy's sister

Mia has math homework tonight, and some nights she is stuck on it. When she is, that is a fact the house can hold the way it holds any other: spoken once, in her mother's words, told on her behalf, and there when the evening is planned. Like Timmy, she is in the record as herself, not as an errand in a parent's list.

## June, the Tuesday helper

June is not family and does not live in the house. She sits with Margaret in the second unit on Tuesday afternoons, and she is part of the household's circle for exactly that: she arrives, the record notes it, and the by-task household notes tell her the few things that matter for the job in front of her. She stands for the wider circle every household has: the people a household authorizes are not only the people who sleep under its roof.
